Most small business owners don't lose money because of one big bookkeeping disaster. They lose it slowly, through small habits that compound over a year — and only notice when tax season or a loan application forces them to actually look at the books.

Here are the five mistakes that show up most often.

## 1. Mixing business and personal expenses

The single most common issue. Every personal purchase run through the business account makes your P&L less accurate, makes tax prep more expensive, and makes it harder to know your real margins. A dedicated business account and card, used consistently, solves most of this on its own.

## 2. Categorizing transactions inconsistently

If the same type of expense gets categorized three different ways depending on who entered it, your reports become unreliable. A clean, stable chart of accounts — set up once and followed consistently — is what makes your P&L actually mean something month over month.

## 3. Skipping monthly reconciliation

Reconciliation isn't just a formality. It's the process that catches duplicate charges, missed transactions, and bank fees before they pile up. Businesses that only reconcile at tax time are usually looking at 10-12 months of accumulated small errors at once.

## 4. Treating bookkeeping as a once-a-year task

Books that are only touched in March, right before taxes, can't tell you anything useful in June. Bookkeeping only pays off as a management tool when it's current — ideally closed out within the first two weeks of the following month.

## 5. Not separating cash flow from profit

A business can be profitable on paper and still run out of cash — usually because of timing between when expenses are paid and when revenue is collected. Your P&L and your cash position are two different questions, and both need to be tracked.

---

None of these fixes require expensive software or a full-time hire. They require a consistent monthly process — which is exactly what a reliable bookkeeping partner should be handling for you.

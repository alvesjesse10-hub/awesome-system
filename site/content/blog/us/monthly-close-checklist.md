A "monthly close" sounds like something only large companies do. In practice, it's just a short, repeatable checklist that keeps your books current — and it matters just as much for a five-person business as it does for a fifty-person one.

Here's a version that works well for most small businesses using QuickBooks Online.

## 1. Reconcile every account

Every bank account and credit card should be reconciled against the actual statement. If the ending balance in QuickBooks doesn't match the bank, something was missed, duplicated, or miscategorized.

## 2. Review the categorization of new transactions

Scan the month's transactions for anything sitting in "Uncategorized" or a generic catch-all category. Left unresolved, these quietly distort your P&L.

## 3. Confirm outstanding invoices and bills

Check accounts receivable for invoices that are overdue, and accounts payable for bills that are due soon. This is also your chance to catch invoices that were never sent.

## 4. Review the P&L against last month

A month-over-month comparison is often the fastest way to catch an error — a cost that suddenly doubled, or revenue that unexpectedly dropped, usually points to either a real business change or a bookkeeping mistake worth investigating.

## 5. Check the balance sheet for anything unusual

Negative balances in accounts that should never be negative, or balances that haven't moved in months, are common signs of an unresolved bookkeeping issue.

## 6. Lock the period

Once everything checks out, close the books for the month in QuickBooks Online. This prevents accidental edits to historical data and gives you a clean, dependable set of financials.

---

Done consistently, this checklist takes a couple of hours a month and means your financials are always current — ready for a loan application, a tax filing, or simply the moment you want to know exactly where your business stands.
